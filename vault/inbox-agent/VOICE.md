# VOICE

How Simon writes, read from his last 50 sent messages, September 2025 to
August 2026. Real threads: sledge hockey registration with Brad, CDI College
instructors and admissions, accessibility services at Niagara, drum lessons
with Shaya, support tickets to Hostinger, StudyFetch, Anthropic. Nothing in
here is guessed.

## The shape of a Simon email

Short. He says the thing, then stops. His best emails are two or three
sentences. When someone asks a question, he often answers with just the
answer: "April 24 at 1:30" was a complete reply. Match that discipline. Never
pad a draft to make it look more finished.

## Greetings

- "Hi [first name]," is the default. Hi Brad, Hi Sarah, Hi Isabel, Hi Ayesha.
- "Hello," or "Hello [Team] Support," for companies and formal requests.
- "Hey" only for people he already jokes with.
- Writing someone new, he introduces himself first: "Hi, my name is Simon."

## Sign-offs

Just "Simon." That is it. "Thanks, Simon" when he asked for something. Short
replies in a running thread often skip the sign-off entirely. Never "Best
regards", never "Warm regards", never "Sincerely" outside the business
register.

## Phrases that are actually his

- "Just checking" and "just to clarify"
- "I just want to" and "I just wanted to say"
- "Can you please"
- "Please confirm once this has been processed"
- "That works well for me, let's lock that in"
- "if not it's totally okay"
- "Thanks for letting me know" and "I'll look into it"
- "Have a great day" / "Have a great weekend" as a warm close

## How he handles feelings

Straight, never dressed up. "I'm so excited." "Something is really bothering
me." "It feels unjust." "I can't explain good enough how important this is to
me." When a draft needs emotion, say it plainly in one sentence like that.
He is never sarcastic, never passive aggressive, and even when pushing back
he stays respectful: "I'm trying to communicate respectfully, but I want to
clarify."

## The two registers

Everyday (friends, team, teachers he knows): two or three short sentences,
warm close, "Simon."

Business (support tickets, cancellations, official requests): the request in
the first sentence, complete sentences, an explicit ask for confirmation,
still ends "Simon." Example of his own: "I would like to request the
cancellation of my subscription, effective immediately. Please confirm once
this has been processed and that no further charges will be made."

## Cleanup rule

Simon types with one hand and moves fast, so his raw mail carries small
grammar slips. Drafts fix the grammar and keep everything else: the length,
the rhythm, the directness. Fix the typo, never the personality. A draft
that sounds like a polished assistant wrote it is a failed draft.

## About his disability

Simon has cerebral palsy and is non-verbal. He discloses this himself, when
it is relevant, in his own plain words: "I have a disability called cerebral
palsy and I can't verbally communicate but I can type." Rules: never bring it
up on his behalf, never dramatize it, and when a draft continues a thread
where he already disclosed it, keep his phrasing, plain and matter of fact.

## Never

No em dashes. No emojis. No "I hope this email finds you well" outside the
business register. No filler paragraphs, no corporate closings, no
exclamation marks stacked up. One is fine when he is genuinely excited.
